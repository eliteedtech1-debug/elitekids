# Game-chain Story Example: Maya's Market Basket

## 1. Teaching purpose

**Class:** Kindergarten (`KG2`)

**Subject:** Numeracy / Mathematics Skill

**Lesson objective:** The child represents a quantity in different ways, combines two small groups, and chooses the total in a familiar market story.

**Concrete experience before the game:** The teacher gives Maya's Market Basket two groups of safe counters: 2 oranges and 3 oranges. Children touch each counter once, join the groups, and say or show that there are 5 altogether.

This is **one lesson/unit objective**, but it needs several game types because each game type develops a different part of the objective. The `game-chain` keeps these parts together and plays them in the teacher's intended order.

## 2. Story

> Maya is helping her grandmother prepare a fruit basket at the market. First, she finds the fruit cards. Then she puts each fruit in the right basket. Next, she rebuilds the fruit picture, counts the groups, and finally tells Grandma how many fruits are in the basket. Can you help Maya, one challenge at a time?

The story is not five unrelated games. Every challenge answers the same question:

> **How can I recognise, organise and combine groups of fruit to find the total?**

## 3. Ordered chain components

| Order | Story challenge | Game type | Learning role | Playable items |
|---:|---|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Find the fruit partners | `matching` | Pair each numeral with its quantity picture | 5 logical pairs |
| 2 | Rebuild Maya's fruit picture | `puzzle-split` | Reconstruct the organised quantity scene | 6 puzzle pieces in the selected level |
| 3 | Choose the right fruit group | `tap-recognition` | Recognise the requested quantity from visual groups | 5 target objects |
| 4 | Put the basket in order | `drag-sort` | Sort fruit groups into the correct quantity baskets | 5 sortable items |
| 5 | Tell Grandma the total | `quiz` | Apply the representation to a simple joining story | 5 questions |

The chain moves from **association → construction → recognition → organisation → application**. It is one lesson-level container, not five separate weekly lessons.

## 4. Suggested round content

### Round 1 — Matching: Find the fruit partners

Match each numeral to its structured quantity card:

```text
1 ↔ one mango
2 ↔ two oranges
3 ↔ three bananas
4 ↔ four apples
5 ↔ five grouped counters
```

Use **5 logical pairs**. The two visual sides of each pair are not counted as two learning objectives.

### Round 2 — Puzzle-split: Rebuild Maya's fruit picture

The child rebuilds one picture showing two groups of fruit: 2 oranges and 3 oranges. The completed picture makes the two groups visible and shows the total group of 5.

Use a 2 × 3 level with **6 pieces**. The puzzle pieces are a construction mechanic; they do not introduce a new mathematical objective.

If the puzzle is too hard for a child, the teacher can use a lower difficulty with the same image and representation. The chain does not shuffle difficulty levels or reveal the completed picture as the answer.

### Round 3 — Tap-recognition: Choose the right fruit group

Maya asks: “Can you find the group with four apples?” The child taps the visual group that represents the named quantity.

Use **5 meaningful visual targets**. Show objects, dots, fingers or groups; do not show a crowded row that creates unnecessary counting load.

### Round 4 — Drag-sort: Put the basket in order

The child sorts five quantity cards into baskets labelled **1**, **2**, **3**, **4** and **5**. Each card has a clear group representation and a correct basket ID.

Use **5 sortable quantity cards** and no meaningless distractors. The child may point or use an adapted tap alternative when dragging is a motor barrier.

### Round 5 — Quiz: Tell Grandma the total

Use five short, picture-supported joining questions:

```text
Maya has 2 oranges. Grandma gives her 1 more. How many altogether?
Maya has 1 banana. She finds 2 more. How many bananas?
Maya puts 2 apples and 2 apples together. How many apples?
Maya has 3 mangoes and gets 1 more. How many mangoes?
Maya has 2 oranges and 3 oranges. How many fruits altogether?
```

Read each question aloud and keep the answer choices concrete and picture-backed where possible.

## 6. Teacher observation

Observe the child across the chain, not only at the final quiz:

- pairs a numeral with a quantity;
- rebuilds or points to a structured quantity;
- recognises a group without recounting unnecessarily;
- sorts a representation into the matching numeral basket;
- explains the total by speaking, pointing, moving counters, signing, drawing or using AAC.

A child may pause between components. A wrong answer should invite another representation or a concrete counter activity, not shame or forced speed.

## 7. Progression rule

The component order is mandatory for the lesson experience:

```text
matching → puzzle-split → tap-recognition → drag-sort → quiz
```

The next **unit** in the wider series remains locked until the current unit's required game-chain has been passed according to the unit gate. The chain's internal rounds are not confused with the series' unit locks.

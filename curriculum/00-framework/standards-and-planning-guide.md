# Common Standards and Planning Guide

## 1. Developmental domains

All five classes should plan for the whole child. A weekly subject label is only an organising
convenience; teachers should observe development across these domains:

| Domain | What to notice |
|---|---|
| Physical / motor | posture, balance, locomotor movement, fine-motor control, safe tool use |
| Communication and language | listening, turn-taking, gesture, vocabulary, home-language and English expression |
| Cognitive / early learning | attention, memory, matching, sorting, quantity, problem solving, curiosity |
| Social-emotional | attachment, self-regulation, confidence, empathy, cooperation and independence |
| Creative / aesthetic | music, rhythm, drawing, construction, pretend play and movement |
| Health, safety and wellbeing | hygiene, nutrition, rest, safe boundaries and recognising trusted adults |

## 2. Subject taxonomy used in this library

The taxonomy follows NERDC's published Nursery subject list while adding explicit care and
play-based domains for younger children.

- `Sensory and Discovery`
- `Communication and Early Literacy`
- `Pre-writing / Writing Skill`
- `Numeracy / Mathematics Skill`
- `Pre-science and Nature`
- `Social and Emotional Learning / Social Habits`
- `Health, Safety and Self-care / Health Habits`
- `Physical Development and Movement`
- `Creative Arts, Music, Rhymes and Pretend Play`
- `Digital Readiness` (optional, supervised, never a substitute for real play)

## 3. Progression by class

### Crèche (0–2)
- No expectation of formal reading, writing or counting.
- Offer sensory exploration: touch, sound, light, colour, movement and object permanence.
- Use one-step language, gestures, imitation and repeated routines.
- Count naturally during songs and play without requiring a correct answer.
- Assessment: brief observations, photos/work samples only with consent, and caregiver notes.

### Playgroup (2–3)
- Name/point to common colours, shapes, body parts, family members and animals.
- Join simple songs, match identical objects and follow two related routine steps.
- Count small groups through action and one-to-one touching, usually 1–3.
- Make marks freely; do not demand letter formation.
- Assessment: show, point, match, imitate and speak with or without prompts.

### Nursery 1 (3–4)
- Build oral vocabulary, listening, rhyme and sound awareness before formal decoding.
- Recognise selected letters and environmental print; begin safe pencil/crayon control.
- Count and match quantities 1–5 using real objects.
- Explore simple patterns, classification, self-care and cooperative play.
- Assessment: practical demonstration and oral response; worksheets are optional reinforcement.

### Nursery 2 (4–5)
- Extend counting, numeral recognition and quantity to 10.
- Explore initial sounds, letter names, picture-word relationships and emergent reading.
- Copy basic shapes/patterns and use them in art and construction.
- Ask questions, predict, compare and describe familiar nature/community experiences.
- Assessment: oral, practical and short mark-making tasks with adult observation.

### Kindergarten (5–6)
- Use concrete materials for joining quantities and simple addition within 5.
- Read and build familiar words; compose short oral and emergent written sentences.
- Recognise and extend simple repeating patterns; sort and explain choices.
- Strengthen independence, collaboration, school routines and readiness.
- Assessment: low-stakes demonstrations, oral language, work samples and teacher records.

## 5. Scheme-of-work rules

1. Start each term with settling, relationships and review.
2. Spiral skills: revisit them in a new context rather than teaching once and abandoning them.
3. Pair every symbolic objective with concrete objects, pictures, actions or sounds.
4. Keep the number of new items small; repetition is intentional.
5. Use local, familiar examples: water, leaves, fruits, family routines, animals, market items,
   weather and community helpers.
6. Include movement, music, outdoor play and quiet choices every day.
7. Avoid ranking children publicly. Document progress privately and positively.
8. Include a weekly home connection that does not require expensive materials or internet.
9. Adjust for disability, neurodiversity, language background, health needs and developmental pace.
10. Review each term against the school's approved state scheme and the latest official guidance.

## 6. EliteKids game mapping

| Learning need | Suitable game/activity | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| sensory exposure | sound/colour/shape reveal, guided story | no wrong-answer state for first exposure |
| recognition | tap or matching | 2–3 large choices, audio optional |
| counting | object-to-number matching | touch/count concrete objects first |
| sequence/pattern | drag-sort or physical cards | start with 2-step or repeating patterns |
| emergent literacy | picture-to-word or sound-to-letter | never require unsupported reading |
| simple addition | join two groups of counters, then visual equation | Kindergarten only; within 5 initially |
| oral sentence | picture sequence and retell | accept speech, gesture, home language and drawing |

All digital content must still pass schema, pedagogy and safety validation. The resources here do
not replace adult supervision or professional assessment.
